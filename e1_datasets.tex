\section{Benchmark datasets}\label{sec:design:data}

We use binary datasets from the KEEL imbalanced collection \citep{alcalafdez2011keel}, read from the files bundled with the \texttt{imbalanced-databases} package. Datasets were included if they (i) originate from the KEEL imbalanced repository, (ii) are binary and (iii) contain at least ten minority instances. Because many KEEL datasets are derived from the same base data (for example, 19 versions of \emph{ecoli} and 14 of \emph{yeast}), at most four datasets per base-data family were kept, chosen deterministically to span the family's range of imbalance ratios; a cap of four is the only one that yields between 50 and 60 datasets. The resulting benchmark contains 55 datasets from 24 families, with 150 to 5472 instances (median 855), 3 to 66 features after encoding, 10 to 559 minority instances and imbalance ratios from 1.82 to 129.44 (median 16.40); 19 datasets have $\mathrm{IR}<9$ and 36 have $\mathrm{IR}\ge 9$ \citep{fernandez2008study}. Nominal attributes (present in 15 datasets) were one-hot encoded, and columns that are constant in the whole dataset were dropped before splitting, which uses no label information; no dataset has missing values. After one-hot encoding and min--max scaling, a mismatch on a nominal attribute with three or more categories adds 2 to the squared Euclidean distance, whereas a numeric attribute adds at most 1, so nominal attributes weigh more in every distance-based sampler, including ours. Twenty-nine datasets contain duplicate rows; they were kept, so identical rows can fall into a training and a test fold, and Section~\ref{sec:results:robustness} repeats the main comparison after removing exact duplicates. Two defects of the package loaders were corrected by parsing the bundled files directly (a leaked class column in \emph{ecoli-0\_vs\_1} and a label-parsing failure in \emph{abalone19}). Table~\ref{tab:datasets} lists the datasets. For each dataset we computed data-complexity measures \citep{ho2002complexity,lorena2020how}, using \texttt{problexity} \citep{komorniczak2023problexity} except for N1 and N2, for which we used reference implementations because the library's versions deviate from the published definitions, and the typology of minority instances into safe, borderline, rare and outlier examples based on five nearest neighbours \citep{napierala2016types}.
